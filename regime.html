How To Use
Trade with regime context, not impulse

Regime is best used as a filter and confirmation layer. It helps you avoid trading into weak conditions and focus your attention when multiple internal factors line up.[file:3]

Suggested reading flow
1
Check the composite regime level. Is the line holding above or below its internal midline? This gives you an instant sense of whether conditions favour buyers, sellers, or a balanced state.
2
Compare against the baseline. When the composite and its moving-average baseline agree on direction, it often signals cleaner trend conditions than when they are fighting each other.
3
Watch for trigger marks. Bullish or bearish trigger markers highlight moments when selected internal confirmations have aligned on the latest confirmed bar, not mid-candle.
4
Respect divergence and strength events. Divergence-style labels and trend-strength markers can act as early warnings around potential exhaustion, regime shifts, or consolidation zones.
5
Align with your price action. Use Regime as a decision layer over your existing system. Enter only when your strategy and regime conditions agree, or stand aside when they conflict.

Best use cases

Market filter for intraday and swing

Signal Context
Example regime states

The following scenarios show how traders typically interpret different combinations of the composite line, baseline, triggers, and strength events. These are illustrative examples only, not fixed rules.[file:3]

Scenario · Strong Bullish Regime
Composite & baseline above midline

Both composite and its moving-average baseline hold firmly above the internal midline, with recurring bullish triggers and occasional trend-strength marks. Traders may prioritise long setups, focus on continuation structures, and avoid counter-trend shorts in this environment.

Scenario · Transitional Regime
Composite crossing baseline

The composite oscillates around the midline or crosses its baseline repeatedly, with mixed trigger events and limited strength markers. Conditions may favour reduced size, shorter holding periods, or simply waiting for a cleaner regime before committing to directional trades.

Scenario · Strong Bearish Regime
Composite & baseline below midline

The composite and baseline stay below the internal midline with repeated bearish triggers and weak attempts to reclaim the midpoint. Traders may give more weight to short setups, resist fading downtrends prematurely, and treat bullish bounces as potential rallies into resistance.

Common questions about Regime

What does Regime Oscillator actually do?
Regime Oscillator provides a composite view of market conditions by combining multiple internal confirmations into a single regime line and baseline. It helps you see whether conditions favour bullish, bearish, or neutral behaviour at a glance, without revealing exact formulas or internal logic.[file:3]
Is this a standalone trading system?
No. Similar to OSOB, Regime Oscillator is a timing and context tool, not a complete strategy.[page:1][file:2] It does not provide fixed entry, exit, or position sizing rules. You should integrate it with your existing setups, risk management, and higher-timeframe structure.
Does Regime repaint or use unfinished bars?
The indicator is designed around confirmed bars for its visual cues and events, so you are not reacting to mid-bar noise or incomplete candles for the main regime logic.[file:3] As with any TradingView indicator, always check how it behaves on your chosen timeframe and symbol before committing real capital.
Which markets and timeframes can I use it on?
Regime works wherever your TradingView data is available — indices, stocks, futures, forex, crypto, and more.[page:1][file:2] You can apply it on intraday charts for scalping and day trading, or on higher timeframes for swing and positional context. Many traders choose a default timeframe and then cross-check with a higher frame for structure.
Is the core logic disclosed?
No. The internal calculation framework, weights, and thresholds are proprietary and are not shared publicly, similar to OpChain and OSOB.[page:1][page:2] Regime is provided as a compiled TradingView indicator for analytical and educational use.
Do I need OpChain to use Regime?
Regime can be used as a standalone indicator on TradingView, but it is also designed to complement the OpChain suite. OpChain users can treat Regime as an additional regime filter over option-chain and price-action context for more disciplined decision-making.[page:2]
